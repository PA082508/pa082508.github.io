Setting up the room tablet — four steps

Done once, by whoever sets the tablet up. The last one repeats every morning and takes a second.

  1. Auto-Lock → Never. Settings → Display & Brightness → Auto-Lock → Never. A tablet that falls asleep hears nothing: the meal song does not play and the banner is not on screen when the window opens.
    If “Never” is missing from the list, Low Power Mode is on — Settings → Battery → turn it off. Low Power Mode forces a 2-minute auto-lock and quietly removes the choice.
  2. Keep it on the charger all day. The screen stays on from morning to close, and that drains a battery by lunchtime. Brightness is yours to set — comfortable for the room is correct; nothing in the system depends on it.
  3. Pin the app with Guided Access. Settings → Accessibility → Guided Access → on; set a passcode. Then open the app and triple-click the side button to start it. The tablet now shows this app and nothing else — it cannot be swiped away, and a curious three-year-old cannot end up in the App Store.
    Two things follow from this, and they are the whole reason for it: the app must stay in the foreground — sounds and banners only exist on an open screen; and nothing else is opened on the room tablet during the day — no browser tab, no other app. If someone did leave the app, reopen it: the day's marks are safe, they live in the record and not on the screen.
  4. Touch the screen once in the morning. A tablet only allows sound after a human has touched it — that is the tablet's rule, not ours, and it cannot be turned off. Until you touch it, the screen carries a plain banner saying the sound is off and offering to turn it on; one tap anywhere is enough, and it lasts the whole day.

Nothing above is a SafePass setting: these are the tablet's own switches, and they are the difference between a room that hears its meal window and a room that does not.
