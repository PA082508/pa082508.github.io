2 · SafePass — what it is
In one sentence: your duties do not change. SafePass records the work you already do, so that the record protects you, the family and the center. This is systematization, not a new way of working.

What SafePass records

SafePass keeps the chain of custody for every child — drop-off, pick-up, early and late care, and transport. Each tap is a timestamp with a name on it. Everything else follows from one rule: physical handoff only. Responsibility transfers when the child is physically in your hands (Accept), or in the authorized person's hands (Release) — never when a parent merely walks into the building.

The four rules

  • Physical handoff only. The transfer happens at the handoff, not at the door of the building.
  • Never tap in advance. Your tap is your signature. Tapping before the child is in your hands signs for something that has not happened.
  • A person you do not recognize. Ask for a government photo ID. If they are not on the authorized list — do not release the child, and call your director.
  • Children are never without an adult. If you are the only checked-in adult in the room, the tablet will not let you check out until someone relieves you, and it tells you how many children are with you. In a genuine emergency there is a way out — the button says so plainly, and the director is told.

Your PIN, and your hours

Your director gives you a personal 4-digit PIN. It is your signature key on any classroom tablet — it is what makes a record yours and not the tablet's. Your worked hours are recorded and visible to you.

  • Check in with your PIN when you start — one drop sound means it is recorded, with the minute.
  • Check out with your PIN when you leave — two drops.
  • Breaks go through check-out and check-in, the same two taps. A break you did not check out of is time you were on the clock, and the record cannot tell the difference.
  • Only your own taps count. A colleague cannot check you in. Nobody types your hours in afterwards, and nobody can quietly round them.
  • Covering another room? Check in with your PIN on that room's tablet. Everything you mark there is recorded under your name, not under the name of the person you are covering.

Meals — nothing changes

Mark meals in Meal Count exactly as you do today. SafePass does not touch the meal count; the two live side by side.

Paper never stops working

The paper sign-in / sign-out panel stays, and stays legal. If the network is down, or a parent has no phone, use paper as before and the office reconciles it. SafePass never removes the paper option.

Photos, and what is not done with them

Every authorized adult is photographed at their own registration, with their own consent. The photo exists for exactly one purpose: so that the teacher at the door can see the face of the person collecting the child. It is kept privately inside our management system, visible only to staff for identification, and it is deleted if that person's access is withdrawn or if they ask us to remove it. It is never used publicly — not on the website, not in social media — without asking that person separately. No face-recognition technology is used at any point: the comparison is made by a person looking at a face.

An authorized adult who prefers not to be photographed keeps their right to collect the child — teachers ask for a photo ID instead. A teacher may ask any authorized person they do not personally recognize for a photo ID.

Adults without a smartphone — the personal QR

An authorized adult who does not use a smartphone — a grandparent, for example — is given a personal QR code, printed as a card or saved as a picture on any phone. At pick-up you scan the code with the classroom tablet, the adult's photo and name appear, and you confirm face to face — the same check as for everyone else. The code is personal, can be replaced at any time, and is deactivated immediately if it is lost.

The code carries a term, and there are three:

  • Permanent — for a regularly authorized adult, valid until it is revoked.
  • For a period — for a named stretch of time, e.g. a grandparent staying for a month.
  • One-time — for a single pick-up by someone new, arranged with the director.

Whatever the term, the final check is always the teacher: the code opens the record, your eyes close it. A scan never releases a child on its own.

Adults without a smartphone are given a personal photo card — the cards come as a batch and are activated at the desk. The check at the door does not change: you see the person, you see their photo, and a card alone never releases a child.

Why this protects you

Ohio licensing rules already require everything above — OAC 5180:2-12-09 (continuous supervision) and OAC 5180:2-12-18 (record keeping). What changes is only how compliance is proven. Until now it meant scattered papers. Now every mark carries the name of the person who made it: your careful work is visible as yours, and no one else's mistake lands on you.

Nap-time breaks follow OAC 5180:2-12-20(A)(7), effective 2026-07-01 — see the one-page guide below.

3 · Your one-page guide

1. Check in when you enter the room

Tap Check in on the room tablet and enter your PIN. That is what puts your name on the tablet for the day. Tap Check out when you leave. Covering a room today? Check in to that room — your check-in follows you, not the tablet.

The first person checked in for the shift is shown as the duty teacher — first and larger in the list. That is ordering only: anyone checked in may sign a handoff.

2. A handoff: card, then your own name

When a parent taps Drop Off or Pick Up, a card appears in Incoming requests with the child's name and photo.

  1. Tap the child's card — that is the child you are handing over.
  2. Tap your own name tile — one big button per teacher checked into this room today.

The person who actually received the child taps their own tile. That single tap is both "it's me" and "confirmed", and the record is signed with your name — not the duty teacher's, not whoever is holding the tablet.

Two steps, never one: card → tile. That is deliberate, so a stray touch cannot release a child.

At pick-up, look at the adult first. The tablet shows you that adult's photo — it helps, it does not decide. Receive or release the child physically, and only then tap.

3. Nobody checked in? Emergency PIN

If no one has checked into the room today, the tablet does not lock you out. It offers "No teacher checked in — emergency confirm with staff PIN". Your PIN identifies you, the handoff goes through, and the record is stamped so the director can see it happened outside the normal path. Use it when it is real — a dead check-in tablet with a parent at the door — not as a shortcut.

4. If you don't recognize the adult

The child stays with you. Call the desk; the desk calls the parent. A parent can authorize the person right from their phone in a minute — or tell us this person was never sent. Two outcomes, both clean: authorized → they collect with that one-time note and photo ID; not authorized → the answer at the door is final and the director is told. You never decide alone at the door, and the door never opens on a guess.

5. Paper panel — the fallback that always works

The paper sign-in / out panel stays on the tablet and stays legal. Network down: use paper as before, and the office reconciles it.

This is an internal tool — it is never offered to families as their path. A family without a smartphone gets a personal card with a QR code from the desk; the paper panel is what you reach for when the network is down, not what you tell a parent to use.

6. Registering a parent from your room

The Parents tab shows the authorized adults for your class only. Anyone not yet registered has a Register button — tap it while the parent is standing in front of you, then tap your own name tile so the action is recorded as yours.

You cannot add a person to the pickup list or remove one from your tablet — who may collect a child is decided at the office, on the enrollment form. You only turn on app access for someone already on the list.

7. Breaks and the start of your shift

Breaks follow the posted schedule.

Checking in before your scheduled start records your arrival; your shift begins at the scheduled time. The tablet writes down when you actually arrived — that is what it is for — and the schedule is what your day is counted against.

8. Lunch breaks during nap time

Ohio lets a room run on fewer adults during nap — within limits. Your partner can take their break without a floater covering the room, as long as all four are true:

  • every child in the group is resting on a cot or a mat;
  • it lasts no longer than two hours;
  • the group has no infants in it;
  • your group is inside the doubled ratio for its age — doubling the ratio is not the same as "one adult, any number of children" — and there are still enough staff in the building to meet the normal ratio for the group.

Check out when you leave and check in when you come back. The tiles then show who is really in the room, which is the whole point of them. One tile during nap is a normal state, not an error. (OAC 5180:2-12-20(A)(7), effective 2026-07-01.)

Setting up the room tablet — four steps · edition v1

Done once, by whoever sets the tablet up. The last one repeats every morning and takes a second.

  1. Auto-Lock → Never. Settings → Display & Brightness → Auto-Lock → Never. A tablet that falls asleep hears nothing: the meal song does not play and the banner is not on screen when the window opens.
    If “Never” is missing from the list, Low Power Mode is on — Settings → Battery → turn it off. Low Power Mode forces a 2-minute auto-lock and quietly removes the choice.
  2. Keep it on the charger all day. The screen stays on from morning to close, and that drains a battery by lunchtime. Brightness is yours to set — comfortable for the room is correct; nothing in the system depends on it.
  3. Pin the app with Guided Access. Settings → Accessibility → Guided Access → on; set a passcode. Then open the app and triple-click the side button to start it. The tablet now shows this app and nothing else — it cannot be swiped away, and a curious three-year-old cannot end up in the App Store.
    Two things follow from this, and they are the whole reason for it: the app must stay in the foreground — sounds and banners only exist on an open screen; and nothing else is opened on the room tablet during the day — no browser tab, no other app. If someone did leave the app, reopen it: the day's marks are safe, they live in the record and not on the screen.
  4. Touch the screen once in the morning. A tablet only allows sound after a human has touched it — that is the tablet's rule, not ours, and it cannot be turned off. Until you touch it, the screen carries a plain banner saying the sound is off and offering to turn it on; one tap anywhere is enough, and it lasts the whole day.

Nothing above is a SafePass setting: these are the tablet's own switches, and they are the difference between a room that hears its meal window and a room that does not.

Setting the adult's PIN — four digits they say themselves

⭐ New with the PIN at the door. The PIN is how an adult who arrived without a phone opens a request at the tablet. Open the child's card → SafePass tab → Set PIN next to the adult's name.

  1. Ask them to say four digits. Do not choose the digits for them: a PIN chosen for someone is a PIN written on a piece of paper in their bag.
  2. Type the four digits and save. If the screen says those digits are already in use in this centre, ask for four different ones — two people cannot share a PIN, or the teacher would see the wrong face at the door.
  3. Tell them what it is for: at the tablet they tap PIN at the door, enter their four digits, and the teacher sees their name and photo and hands the child over face to face. The PIN opens a request — it never opens the door by itself.

No photo on file? The door will ask the teacher to check ID. Take the photo at the desk while the adult is standing in front of you.

Registering a parent and installing the app — one minute

The family should not have to learn anything. They come to you; you do these five things and hand the phone back. Everything below happens on their phone, not on ours.

  1. Register them. Take the photo and the phone number on your own screen — the photo is what a teacher sees at pick-up, so take it here, not "later".
  2. Open the Parent App on their phone. Scan the QR from the parent letter or type the centre's link. Nothing is installed from an app store.
  3. Add to Home Screen so it opens like an app.
    📷 Temporary drawing. Replace with a real screenshot from the phone.
  4. Enter their number once. The screen says “Enter your registered phone number” and under the box “Use the number Play Academy has on file for pickup.” Type it, tap Continue →. No code is sent and there is no password — registering the number is what activates it.
  5. Wait for the green ✓ and hand the phone back. Their children are on the screen; that is the whole setup. Tell them the only thing they need to remember: two taps a day.

If it says “This number is not registered yet” — the registration in step 1 has not saved, or the number differs by a digit. Fix it on your screen and try again; do not send the family home to "try later".

No smartphone? Skip steps 2–4: they get a personal card with a QR code — the same security, the same one-tap process.

4 · Assignments outside your classroom

Some days the administration sends you to another room — covering a colleague, a walk-round, an extra pair of hands at a busy hour. The rules do not change when the room does.

The same PIN, in the other room

Check in with your own PIN on that room's tablet. Everything you mark there — handoffs, meals, hours — is recorded under your name, not under the name of the person you are covering. Check out when you leave that room.

Responsibility and signature travel with you

A handoff you confirm in another room is your signature exactly as it is in your own: you looked at the adult, you handed the child over, you tapped. The room changes; the rule that the person who received the child taps their own tile does not.

Taking a group as a whole

Accept my group — taking responsibility for a whole group in one action — is in development and not on the tablets yet. Until it ships, do what you do today: check in to the room and confirm each handoff as it comes. We are not describing a button that does not exist.

5 · What the parent sees

Families get their own package, and it is worth knowing what is in it: parents ask you, not the office, and an answer of "I don't know" at the door costs the whole minute you saved.

Everyone with the right registers — including emergency contacts

Everyone a family authorizes — including emergency contacts — registers once at the desk: a photo and a phone number (or a card). One minute, once. Being named on the enrolment form is what gives the right; registering is what gives you a face to check at the door. Until someone on that list has registered, they are released on government photo ID and the record says “ID checked, not yet registered” — and the desk asks them to register while they are standing there.

At the desk, once

The first time, an adult is registered at the front desk: name, phone, and a photo taken right there. That photo is what you will see on the tablet at pick-up. Anyone the family names as allowed to collect the child is registered the same way — the family decides who, the document says so, and the desk records it.

On their own phone, after that

The parent opens the app and enters the phone number the desk registered — and that is the whole sign-in. No code is sent to them and there is no password: registering the number at the desk is what activates it, so the number itself is the key. They see their own children — nobody else's — and they can hand off and collect. If a parent tells you “it asked me for a code”, they tapped the small second line meant for a code the desk hands out; the ordinary way in is the number alone.

What they do at the door

Drop-off: the parent taps the child's name; you accept, and the child is in your count from that second. Pick-up: the parent taps, you see who is asking and their photo, you hand the child over and confirm. The confirmation is yours and carries your name — the parent's tap is a request, your tap is the record.

An adult without a smartphone

Nobody is locked out. The desk gives them a personal card with a QR code; the check at the door is the same either way.

What the family is NOT asked

No signature is required from families for SafePass itself, and no payment. If a parent says they were asked to sign something for it, that is a mistake worth telling your director about the same day.

The honest part

Not every family will have activated their phone on day one. This is a transition period; the administration will announce the dates by which every family is activated. Until then the teacher hands the child over the usual way — in person, face to face, to the adult they know — and the family is registered at the desk when they next come in.

6 · Questions & answers

Who may collect a child — the three cases

1 · Permanent right — parents and emergency contacts ticked as allowed to collect on the enrolment form. Everyone in this group registers once at the desk (photo + phone, or a card). You check the person against the photo the system shows you.

2 · A one-time note — only for someone who is not in the child's record at all. The parent authorizes them from their phone; there is no photo on file, so you check a government photo ID against the name in the note.

3 · Everyone else — the child stays with you, and you call the desk (see your one-page guide). Nothing is decided at the door by guessing.

If someone with a permanent right has not registered yet, they are released on government photo ID and the record says “ID checked, not yet registered” — and the desk asks them to register while they are standing there.

Can a family opt out of SafePass?

The phone is optional; the record is not. A family without a smartphone uses a personal card with a QR code from the desk — same check, same single tap.

Does anything change in what I do?

No. Greet each child, hand each child only to the right adult, keep count of your room, mark meals. The work is the same; what is new is that it is written down under your name.

Is this a way of watching me?

It is the opposite. Every mark carries the name of the person who made it, so your careful work is visible as yours — and no one else's mistake can land on you. Your worked hours come from your own two taps, are recorded and are visible to you; nobody types them in afterwards and nobody can quietly round them.

Will the tablet show me rates or amounts?

No. SafePass records and shows worked hours — nothing else. There are no rates, no totals in money and no calculation of any kind on the tablet.

Is my PIN only for SafePass?

Your PIN is your identity in every teacher application — the cabinet today and every module added tomorrow. One PIN, one person, everywhere in the App. It is the single way you confirm who you are when you open anything meant for you, which is exactly why it is not shared and not written on the tablet stand.

What exactly is my PIN?

A personal 4-digit number your director gives you. It is your signature key on any classroom tablet. It is what makes a record yours. Do not share it — a colleague cannot check you in, and should not be able to.

I forgot to check out yesterday. What happens?

The record says you were still on duty, because that is all it can say. Tell your director — corrections are made at the office, never by editing the tablet.

Do I check out for lunch?

Yes. Breaks go through check-out and check-in, the same two taps. A break you did not check out of is time you were on the clock, and the record cannot tell the difference.

I am covering another teacher's room today.

Check in with your PIN on that room's tablet. Your name appears in "In this room today", and everything you mark there — meals, handoffs — is recorded under your name, not the name of the person you are covering. Check out when you leave.

I am the only adult in the room and my shift is over.

The tablet will not let you check out while children are in the room, and it tells you how many. That is the supervision rule, not a quirk. In a real emergency the button "Leave anyway — the director will be told" exists: nobody holds you in the building, the system simply records honestly that this was an exception.

A parent is at the door but no card appeared.

Check the room selector at the top — you may be looking at another room. If there is still no request, have them tap Drop Off / Pick Up. If they cannot, take the child in person and tell the director — the record is made at the office.

Nobody is checked in and a parent is waiting.

Use the emergency PIN path — the tablet offers it. The handoff goes through, and the record is marked as taken outside the normal path so the director can see it. It is for real situations, not for saving a tap.

I do not recognize the adult at pick-up.

Ask for a government photo ID. If the person is not on the authorized list, do not release the child and call your director. The tablet's photo helps you; the decision is still yours, face to face.

Can I add someone to a child's pickup list?

No, and that is deliberate. Who may collect a child is decided at the office, on the enrollment form. From your tablet you can only turn on app access for someone already on that list.

What if the network is down?

Use the paper sign-in / out panel. It stays on the tablet, it stays legal, and the office reconciles it.

A parent or grandparent has no smartphone.

Nothing breaks. They get a personal QR code — a printed card, or a picture saved on any phone. You scan it with the tablet, their photo and name come up, and you confirm face to face, exactly as with everyone else. Codes come in three terms: permanent, for a period, or one-time for a single pick-up arranged with the director.

The cards come as a batch and are activated at the desk; until a particular person has theirs, they are released on government photo ID with the record marked “ID checked, not yet registered”. The door check does not change either way — you see the person, and a card alone never releases a child.

Does this change Meal Count?

No. Mark meals exactly as today.

Am I obliged to sign this acknowledgment electronically?

No. Signing electronically is voluntary — you may print this package and sign it by hand, or sign it in person at the office. Paper and electronic signatures are equally accepted. The signature confirms you were properly informed; it adds no new obligation.

When does my room start?

One room goes first; your director will tell you the day your room joins.

Who do I ask?

Your director, any day.
