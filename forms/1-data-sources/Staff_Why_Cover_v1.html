A note for the team

Why your mark matters

For years you have done this duty faithfully. Licensing required a paper sheet, and you kept it — every arrival, every departure, written by hand, on time. That paper mattered, but let's be honest about what it did: it recorded information. It sat in a folder. Nothing ran on it.

That changes now. The same mark — a child arrived, a child left — is no longer just a record. It is the start signal for the whole operation. The moment you tap a child in, the system goes to work: meal counts are planned from it, food purchases are sized by it, staffing and payroll calculations depend on it, our funding claims are built on it. One tap, and everything downstream runs correctly.

Which also means: a missing mark is no longer a blank cell on paper. If a child is in your room and not marked in, the system is now working with wrong facts — meals get planned for the wrong number of children, reports go out incomplete, and the errors cost real money and real trust.

The meal mark carries the same weight. Until now, you marked meals on the kitchen worker's iPad when food was delivered. From now on, you mark them on your own classroom iPad — one tap, at the moment the meal is served. Every meal claim stands on that mark; a missed mark means a served meal that can't be counted or paid.

Nothing about your duty is new. What's new is its weight. You are not filling out a form anymore — you are starting the engine of the whole program. Same hands, same care, same moment at the door. It just matters more now — because now, everything listens.

One more thing, and it matters: the electronic mark does not replace the paper sheet yet. For now, we keep both — tap on the tablet, and keep the paper sheet exactly as before. This parallel period is deliberate and it is short: we need a stretch of clean, complete electronic records to take to the licensing authorities and make the case — together, with your work as the proof — that the paper can be retired. Every day both are kept correctly brings that day closer.

This first week (through Saturday, September 27) is a trial period. Mistakes are expected and allowed — we will find and fix them together. If anything is unclear or looks wrong, just ask the administration — that is what this week is for. Starting Monday, September 28, electronic attendance becomes mandatory for everyone.

This letter is to read — there is nothing to sign here.
